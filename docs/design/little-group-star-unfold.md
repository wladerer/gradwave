# Star-unfolding for symmetry-breaking perturbations (little group of q)

Design note for extending the symmetry-reduced density response from the
*totally-symmetric* subset it handles today to *symmetry-breaking* perturbations
— phonons at a wavevector **q**, single atomic displacements, single-site
substitutions, and general (non-isotropic) strains. This is the response-side
counterpart of using the irreducible Brillouin zone in the forward SCF, and the
enabling machinery is the **little group of q** and the **star of q**.

## Where we are

The forward SCF reduces k-points to the point-group IBZ and symmetrizes the
density each step (`symmetry.RhoSymmetrizer`), 10–15× faster on symmetric cells.
The *backward* (response) side reduces only for perturbations that preserve the
full crystal group:

- `scf.implicit.apply_chi0` / `solve_adjoint` / `density_loss_param_grads` gained
  an `assume_totally_symmetric` opt-in: for a totally symmetric perturbation the
  response δρ = χ₀w is itself totally symmetric, so the IBZ k-sum (weights = star
  multiplicities) folded by the scalar `RhoSymmetrizer` reproduces the full-BZ
  response. nspin=1 and nspin=2 (collinear).
- `scf.alchemical` reuses the same fold for a homogeneous (point-group-preserving)
  substitution.
- `postscf.dielectric` handles the one non-trivial special case: a **polar vector
  at q=0** (the E-field response), via `symmetry.VectorFieldSymmetrizer`.

Everything symmetry-reduced in the response layer is **q=0**. A perturbation that
lowers the symmetry — the exact case phonon DFPT and single-defect design want —
still forces `use_symmetry=False` and the full TR-reduced k-mesh.

## The physics of the general case

A perturbation V^{(q)}(r) with lattice-periodic modulation e^{iq·r} does two
things a totally-symmetric perturbation does not:

1. **It couples k to k+q.** First-order perturbation theory sends a Bloch state
   ψ_{n,k} to a response δψ that lives at **k+q**. The Sternheimer equation
   becomes (H_{k+q} − ε_{n,k}) |δψ_{n,k}⟩ = −(1 − P_{k+q}) V^{(q)} |ψ_{n,k}⟩,
   and χ₀ contracts ⟨ψ_{n,k}| … |δψ_{n,k}⟩ across the two crystal momenta. The
   response density δρ(r) carries the e^{iq·r} modulation and is complex.

2. **It transforms as a representation of the little group of q, not trivially.**
   The **little group** G_q = { g ∈ G : g q ≡ q (mod reciprocal lattice) } is the
   subgroup that leaves q invariant. The remaining cosets generate the **star of
   q**, { q, g₂q, …, g_sq } — the symmetry-equivalent wavevectors. A perturbation
   (a displacement pattern, a strain) decomposes into **small (allowed)
   irreducible representations** Γ_μ of G_q. Only G_q — not the full group —
   symmetrizes the response at q; the rest of the star is generated by unfolding.

The reduction that makes this cheaper:

- **k-points** reduce to the IBZ of **G_q** (larger than the full-group IBZ, but
  still a real saving), with the k↔k+q pairing bookkeeping.
- **The perturbation set** (3·N_atom displacements for a phonon) reduces to the
  irreducible displacements under G_q; the rest are generated by the group action.
- **The response** at each q is symmetrized by a **q-dependent field
  symmetrizer** built from G_q (the generalization of `RhoSymmetrizer` /
  `VectorFieldSymmetrizer`), then the full star is reconstructed from the one
  representative q.

For a **totally symmetric** perturbation q=0 and G_q = G and Γ_μ is the identity
irrep — the present code is exactly this degenerate case.

## What is missing (the gaps to fill)

1. **Star-of-q and coset machinery.** No construction of {gq} or of point-group /
   little-group cosets. `little_group` exists but is band-only and not exposed to
   the response/symmetry layer.
2. **q-dependent IBZ reduction.** `reduce_mesh` (`symmetry.py:181`) orbits under
   the full group with no q-argument; a phonon at q needs the IBZ of G_q plus the
   k↔k+q pairing.
3. **k+q coupling in the response.** The entire χ₀/Sternheimer sum
   (`_chi0_channel`, `_chi0_channel_metal`) contracts ψ_k with δψ_k at the *same*
   k. There is no k+q Hamiltonian, no k+q G-sphere remap, and `w_r` is a real,
   grid-local field — a q perturbation is complex and grid-modulated. **This is
   the largest single piece.**
4. **A q-dependent field symmetrizer.** `RhoSymmetrizer` builds idx/phase from
   m→mᵀW mod box with no q-umklapp g0(q) and no e^{iq·t} translation phase — the
   latter compounded by the pure-translation dedup in `find_spacegroup`
   (`symmetry.py:82`), which discards exactly the ops the small group of q
   distinguishes.
5. **Small-representation projection.** Only Γ special cases exist (scalar, polar
   vector, axial vector, rank-2 tensor). No general "project a perturbation onto
   irrep Γ_μ of G_q" operator, and no **projective / factor-system** handling
   (only a warning label at `irreps.py:19`), which zone-boundary non-symmorphic
   groups require.
6. **Perturbation reduction by G_q.** Phonon displacement reduction today uses the
   *full point group at Γ* (`phonons.py HessianSymmetry`) or the diagonal-supercell
   group (`phonons_supercell.py`); neither is q-resolved.

## Phased plan

Each phase is independently landable and independently verifiable against the
existing `use_symmetry=False` full-BZ path (the ground-truth oracle throughout).

**Phase 0 — group theory, no physics.** Promote `little_group` into `symmetry.py`;
add `star_of_q` (the coset reps generating {gq}) and `little_group_ibz` (reduce a
k-mesh under G_q with k↔k+q bookkeeping). Revisit the `find_spacegroup`
pure-translation dedup so the e^{iq·t} phases survive. *Verify:* star sizes ×
|G_q| = |G|; IBZ_q orbit counts vs a brute-force full-mesh enumeration; the q=0
case reproduces `reduce_mesh` exactly.

**Phase 1 — q-dependent scalar field symmetrizer.** Generalize `RhoSymmetrizer`
to `QFieldSymmetrizer(q)`: idx/phase built over G_q with the umklapp g0(q) shift
(the `m·Wᵀ + g0` fold already in `irreps.py:139`) and the translation phase.
Start on q values commensurate with the MP mesh. *Verify:* folding a full-BZ
δρ^{(q)} (computed with symmetry off) reproduces it; idempotence; q=0 reduces to
`RhoSymmetrizer`.

**Phase 2 — k+q in the χ₀ / Sternheimer sum (the core).** Add k+q Hamiltonian
construction and the k+q G-sphere remap, a complex grid-modulated perturbation
field, and the ⟨ψ_k|·|δψ_{k+q}⟩ contraction in `_chi0_channel[_metal]`. Keep it
`use_symmetry=False` first (full mesh, no fold) to isolate the k+q mechanics.
*Verify:* against a frozen-phonon finite difference of the density at q on a
supercell (the existing `phonons_supercell` path is the oracle); against the q→0
limit matching the present q=0 response.

**Phase 3 — small-rep projection + fold in the reduced response.** Combine
Phases 1–2: run the χ₀/Sternheimer sum on the IBZ of G_q, project the
perturbation onto its small irrep, fold the response with `QFieldSymmetrizer`,
and unfold the star. Generalize the dielectric two-stage recipe. *Verify:* the
reduced response equals the Phase-2 full-mesh response to solver tolerance; the
speedup ≈ |G_q|-fold.

**Phase 4 — wire into phonon DFPT at q.** Reduce the displacement set by G_q,
drive the Phase-3 reduced response per irreducible (q, displacement), assemble
the dynamical matrix. Non-symmorphic zone-boundary q need the projective /
factor-system handling from Phase 0/1. *Verify:* dispersion matches the supercell
finite-displacement path on a test crystal (e.g. Si, MgO).

## Effort and risk

- **Phase 2 is the crux and the risk.** k+q coupling touches the Hamiltonian,
  the plane-wave basis (different G-sphere at k+q), and the response contraction;
  the perturbation and response become complex. It is a substantial change to the
  response core, gated by the frozen-phonon oracle.
- **Phases 0–1 are self-contained group theory** with cheap, decisive tests — the
  right first slice, and they unblock a q-resolved phonon-displacement reduction
  even before Phase 2 (a modest but real saving on the existing supercell path).
- **The projective-representation handling (non-symmorphic zone boundary) is the
  subtlest correctness trap** — worth isolating and testing against known
  character tables (spglib / a reference table) rather than discovering it inside
  a phonon run.
- **Recommended first commit:** Phase 0 + Phase 1 (`star_of_q`,
  `little_group_ibz`, `QFieldSymmetrizer`), verified purely against the full-BZ
  fold with symmetry off — no response-core change, no regression risk, and it
  turns the largest remaining piece (Phase 2) into a well-scoped, well-oracle'd
  follow-up.

## Implementation status

- **Phase 0 — landed and verified.** `symmetry.little_cogroup`, `star_of_q`, and
  `little_group_ibz` with a group-theory test suite (`tests/unit/test_little_group.py`):
  orbit–stabilizer (`|star| · |G_q| = |G|`), Γ-reduction to `reduce_mesh`, the
  umklapp identity `W⁻ᵀq − q = g0`, star generation, and IBZ sizing.
- **Phase 1 — landed and verified (for ordinary small reps), with the projective
  case handled by an honest fallback.** `symmetry.QFieldSymmetrizer` averages the
  little co-group with the q-shifted non-symmorphic phase, and it is a correct
  idempotent projector wherever the small representation is **ordinary** —
  verified to machine precision on the decisive case (Si `[1/2,0,0]`, whose little
  group carries both glide ops *and* nonzero umklapps), on interior q, on all
  symmorphic bcc-Fe q, and matching `RhoSymmetrizer` exactly at q=Γ. Two things
  had to be right and now are: (a) the **q-shifted band-limit** `{|q+G| ≤ G_cut}`
  (the set the little group preserves), not the ordinary density sphere `{|G| ≤
  G_cut}` — the latter drops a boundary shell for `g0 ≠ 0` and breaks the
  projector; (b) the gather construction `(P c)(m) = ⟨e^{−2πi(q+m)·w} c(Wᵀ(m−g0))⟩`.
  The remaining hard case is the **projective small rep** (non-trivial factor
  system, e.g. Si `[1/4,1/4,0]`): there the plain average is provably not a
  projector, so construction raises fail-fast and the response fold must use the
  full mesh at that q — correct, just not reduced. A full multiplier-rep
  construction that also reduces the projective q's is a further extension.
  Tests: `tests/unit/test_little_group.py`.
- **Phase 2 — bare k+q χ₀ landed and verified (internal oracles).**
  `postscf.dfpt_q.chi0_q` computes the +q Fourier component of the bare density
  response δρ_{+q} = χ₀[δV_q] on a full `time_reversal=False` mesh, with the
  k+q index/umklapp lookup (`kpq_map`) and the cross-sphere product carrying the
  `e^{iG0·r}` umklapp phase. Verified by (a) **q=0 reduction** — `apply_chi0 =
  2·Re(chi0_q)` to <1e-7 (the factor 2 is the c.c. term that at q=0 doubles;
  at q≠0 it is the −q component), which checks the whole k+q plumbing reduces
  correctly; and (b) **q≠0 conjugate symmetry** δρ_{−q} = conj(δρ_q) to <1e-6 at
  q with and without umklapps, which exercises the genuine k↔k+q coupling.
  Tests: `tests/gradcheck/test_dfpt_q.py`. **Still owed:** the external
  gold-standard oracle — a supercell frozen-phonon / static-potential
  finite-difference (`phonons_supercell.dynamical_matrix`) — and the screened
  (self-consistent) response; conjugate symmetry is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Phases 3–4 — not started.** Fold `chi0_q` over the little-group IBZ with
  `QFieldSymmetrizer` and unfold the star (Phase 3), then drive an atomic
  displacement perturbation into the dynamical matrix at q and match the
  supercell dispersion (Phase 4).

## Non-goals

This note does not cover magnetic little groups at q≠0 (spin-wave / magnon
response), spin-spiral (generalized Bloch) boundary conditions, or q-dependent
response for metals beyond the existing smeared-occupation χ₀ window. Each is a
further extension once the non-magnetic q≠0 path exists.
