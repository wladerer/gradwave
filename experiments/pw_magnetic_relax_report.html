Plane-wave validation: magnetic moments & geometry relaxation

Ferromagnetic bcc-Fe and fcc-Ni (collinear spin, nspin=2) and a diamond geometry optimization, all on gradwave's plane-wave path. The magnetic section leads with the CI-validated 63 anchor and then resolves the k-mesh dependence of the Fe moment against Quantum ESPRESSO at matched settings. The headline result: the Fe moment oscillates with k-mesh (a Fermi-surface sampling effect), gradwave reproduces that oscillation against QE to ≤0.01 μB at every mesh and smearing scheme, and the converged moment is 2.30 ± 0.02 μB. FLAPW is out of scope (no spin-polarization).

3. Moment convergence — the k-mesh oscillation

The base grid below sweeps the starting moment and k-mesh at gaussian 0.1 eV. The moment is seed-independent (start_mag 0.4 and 0.7 give the identical self-consistent moment at every mesh), so the 63→83 change (Fe 2.22→2.40 μB, Ni 0.64→0.70) is not a multi-basin/seed effect — it is k-sampling. Pushing Fe to 103 shows the moment does not keep rising but falls back to ~2.22: the moment oscillates with mesh as the k-grid samples the sharp Fe d-states at EF. The three tests below (smearing-scheme sweep, width→0 extrapolation, and a QE cross-check at identical settings) pin the converged value and show gradwave reproduces the oscillation against QE.

4. Spin-resolved DOS — the exchange splitting

Spin-up (blue, plotted up) and spin-down (red, plotted down) densities of states from a Kernel-Polynomial-Method expansion of the converged potential at the anchor settings, aligned to EF = 0. Fe shows a large exchange splitting (the up band sits well below EF while down straddles it); Ni's splitting is small, consistent with its weak ~0.6 μB moment.

5. Geometry optimization — diamond

The second C in diamond starts with its bond to the first C displaced ~0.08 Å off the ideal tetrahedral (a/4)3 geometry; BFGS relaxes it back. Diamond is an insulator with a norm-conserving pseudo, so the forces are clean and the relax converges tightly (fmax → 10-4 eV/Å) in a few steps — a trustworthy example. We report the translation-invariant C–C bond (atom 1 − atom 0); atom 0 is pinned, so the bond and atom 1's absolute site coincide. (The originally planned PtCu L10 metal relax was dropped: its affordable 4×4×4 PAW relax converged to a spurious off-symmetry Cu site from coarse-mesh force noise, and each SCF step ran ~50 min at ecutrho 400 Ry — see caveats.)

6. Honest caveats

  • The code is validated; the mesh dependence is a Fermi-surface oscillation. gradwave reproduces QE's Fe moment at every mesh and smearing scheme (section 3): 63≈2.22, 83≈2.40, 103≈2.22 — the moment oscillates with k-sampling of the sharp Fe d-states at EF, it does not monotonically rise. The 83 value is an oscillation peak, not the converged answer; the trustworthy moment is the width→0, cross-scheme value 2.30 ± 0.02 μB. The 63 CI-fixture agreement is real but partly coincidental (both codes sit near the low point of the oscillation there).
  • Convergence level. Validation-grade. Fe/Ni use ecut 60 Ry on the 1-atom primitive; the k-mesh is swept to 103 for Fe (section 3). A production moment would also converge ecut, push the mesh further to damp the oscillation, and report the smearing scheme explicitly.
  • Smearing. The 0.1 eV gaussian carries an O(width²) finite-temperature error; mp1/cold cancel it to higher order. Notably the k-mesh oscillation is present for all three schemes at width 0.1 (section 3), so it is a Fermi-surface sampling effect, not purely a smearing artifact; the zero-width extrapolation removes the smearing part.
  • Symmetry off for the magnets. A collinear moment breaks the paramagnetic space group, so IBZ reduction / density symmetrization is disabled for Fe and Ni.
  • PtCu was dropped for a diamond relax. The intended PtCu L10 PAW-metal relax proved untrustworthy at an affordable cost: with symmetry: false and a 4×4×4 mesh it "converged" (fmax→3×10-5) but to a spurious Cu site 0.067 Å off the symmetric L10 plane — coarse-mesh force noise — and each PAW SCF step ran ~50 min at ecutrho 400 Ry, so a denser, trustworthy mesh was out of budget. Diamond (insulator, norm-conserving, clean forces) is the honest geometry-optimization demo; it relaxes the displaced C cleanly back to its ideal site.
  • FLAPW excluded. gradwave's all-electron FLAPW path has no spin-polarization, so magnetic moments are not available there. Every magnetic result above is from the plane-wave pseudopotential path exclusively.
